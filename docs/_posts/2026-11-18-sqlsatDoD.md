At the recent [2026 PASS Data Communtiy Summt](https://passdatacommunitysummit.com/) taking place in November, I had a few questions from people about SQL Saturday and Day of Data (as well as Data Saturdays) and what the differences are. I'll try to explain a bit from my perspective here, though others might see things a little differently.

SQL Saturday was the original community event organized for SQL Server professionals. Andy Warren, Brian Knight, and I spun this out of SQL Server Central. Our idea was to bring the conference experience to local communities and user groups. You can read more on the [history](https://dayofdata.org/about/) page. 

We had tremendous support and success from 2007 to 2020. However, with the future uncertain as the PASS organization was failing, a few people got together to form [Data Saturdays](https://datasaturdays.com/), in hope we could keep these events moving forward. I helped, and thought it was a nice step forward.

With Redgate purchasing the assets, and [donating the brand to a non-proft](https://blog.sqlsaturday.com/2021-05-13-Corporation/), US 501(c)(3) corporation, SQL Saturday continued to operate independently, with the support of the community and the guidance of its board of directors.

Both SQL Saturday and Data Saturday continued as brands, with a bit of a split between the communities that supported them. Some organizers like one brand over the other. My main concern was having a central organization to help with sponsorship and coordination, ensuring that events could continue to thrive regardless of the brand preference. 

Both run events that are essentially the same, differing in name. SQL Saturday has had global sponsors, and free PASS Summit tickets from Redgate, that we can offer to organizers. Data Saturday offers tools. I think our way is better, but that's up to everyone to decide for themselves.

## Evolutions
Over the last few years, the "SQL" brand has become a little less popular. The Microsoft Data Platform has expanded far beyond SQL Server. Many data professionals work with a variety of technologies, and more than a few user groups re-branded themselves as "Data Platform" groups.

A few event organizers approached me with two main concerns:
1. "Data" is a better brand than "SQL"
2. Saturday is limiting in terms of scheduling

We settled on Day of Data as a new brand, and acquired the [dayofdata.org](https://dayofdata.org/) domain. Our goal was to offer both brands in 2026 and then evolve to only Day of Data events in 2027.

So far that appears to be going well. We haven't seen any loss of branding or attendance from the name change. Some areas are still struggling to run events, post-pandemic and some are thriving. Sponsorship is hard to come by, as is space, but I'm hopeful we can continue to move forward.

We would love to see more events, and more communities coming together. If you're interested in organizing a Day of Data event, please reach out to us through our website or join our mailing list for updates and guidance.

## Reminders

[SQL Saturday is an independent US 501.c.3 corporation](https://blog.sqlsaturday.com/2021-11-18-nonprofit/) organized as a public charity. We are not owned by, or managed by Redgate Software. Redgate donated the brand to the corporation and gave us a grant to run events, but there is no oversight of the corporation. Redgate does maintain a permanent seat on the board of directors, currently occupied by Steve Jones, but has no more influence than any of the other members of the board.
